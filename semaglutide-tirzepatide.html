How does GLP-1 therapy work?

Appetite control

Mimics the GLP-1 hormone that signals fullness to your brain, so you feel satisfied on less food.

Slower digestion, steadier energy

FAQ

Semaglutide & tirzepatide FAQs

Semaglutide is a single-hormone (GLP-1) receptor agonist. Tirzepatide activates both GLP-1 and GIP receptors, which is why clinical trials show it often produces greater average weight loss. Your provider can help you choose based on your health history and goals.

Both are delivered as a once-weekly self-administered injection using a pre-filled, dial-a-dose pen with a very fine needle. Your care team will walk you through the injection technique at your first visit.

Published clinical trials report average losses of roughly 15% of body weight with semaglutide and up to 20% with tirzepatide over 52–68 weeks of consistent use. Results vary by individual, dose, and adherence — this is not a guarantee.

The most common side effects are mild and gastrointestinal — nausea, stomach upset, diarrhea, or fatigue — especially during dose increases. Most resolve as your body adjusts. Your provider will monitor you and adjust your dose if needed.

GLP-1 medications are generally not recommended for patients with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2, a history of pancreatitis, or who are pregnant or breastfeeding. Your consultation will screen for these and other contraindications.

Your provider will determine whether baseline labs are appropriate based on your health history. Many patients can begin treatment the same day as their consultation if no additional testing is needed.
